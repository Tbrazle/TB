# Understanding Your Emotions

Emotional intelligence isn't about suppressing feelings — it's about understanding and directing them.

<!-- premium -->

## The Emotional Vocabulary Gap

Many men were raised with a limited emotional vocabulary. We learn happy, sad, angry — and that's it. But emotions are nuanced. Expanding your vocabulary helps you understand yourself better.

### Try This
Instead of "I'm stressed," ask yourself:
- Am I overwhelmed (too much to handle)?
- Am I anxious (uncertain about the future)?
- Am I frustrated (blocked from reaching a goal)?
- Am I exhausted (running on empty)?

## The Pause Practice

When you feel a strong emotion, pause for 6 seconds before reacting. This gives your prefrontal cortex time to catch up with your amygdala.

**The 6-second pause:**
1. Notice the physical sensation (tight chest, hot face, clenched jaw)
2. Take a slow breath
3. Ask: "What am I actually feeling?"
4. Ask: "What do I need right now?"

## Emotions Are Data, Not Instructions

Feeling angry doesn't mean you have to act aggressive. Feeling sad doesn't mean you're broken. Emotions are signals — listen to them, but choose your response.

**Reflection exercise:** Pick one strong emotion you felt this week. What was it trying to tell you?
